# note: cellulitis
# synthetic. lower extremity cellulitis, short admission for IV antibiotics.
EMERGENCY DEPARTMENT OBSERVATION DISCHARGE

Diagnosis: cellulitis of the right lower extremity.

You received intravenous cefazolin in the observation unit. The erythema has demarcated and you are suitable for oral therapy at home.

Take cephalexin 500 mg PO QID for 10 days. Complete the full course. Take acetaminophen 650 mg PO q6h PRN pain or fever.

Elevate the extremity above the level of your heart whenever you are seated or lying down. This is as important as the antibiotic.

The border of the erythema has been marked with a pen. Do not wash the mark off. It allows you and your doctor to see whether the infection is advancing.

Do not apply any topical antibiotic ointment. Do not soak the leg.

Follow up with your primary care physician in 2 days so the margin can be reassessed. A wound care referral has been placed if the skin breaks down.

Return to the emergency department immediately if the erythema advances beyond the marked border, if you develop fever above 101 F, if you see purulent drainage or a fluctuant area, if the pain becomes severe and out of proportion to the appearance, or if you notice crepitus or skin necrosis.
