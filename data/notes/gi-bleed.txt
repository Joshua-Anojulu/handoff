# note: gi-bleed
# synthetic. upper GI bleed from NSAID-induced ulcer, endoscopically treated.
DISCHARGE INSTRUCTIONS FOLLOWING UPPER GASTROINTESTINAL HAEMORRHAGE

Diagnosis: bleeding gastric ulcer, NSAID induced. Endoscopic haemostasis achieved. Helicobacter pylori testing positive; eradication therapy prescribed.

MEDICATIONS
- Pantoprazole 40 mg PO BID for 8 weeks, then 40 mg PO QD.
- Amoxicillin 1000 mg PO BID for 14 days.
- Clarithromycin 500 mg PO BID for 14 days.
- Ondansetron 4 mg PO q8h PRN nausea.
- Ferrous sulfate 325 mg PO QD for iron deficiency.

ABSOLUTE RESTRICTIONS
- Do not take ibuprofen, naproxen, aspirin, or any other NSAID. An NSAID caused this bleed and another will cause the next one.
- Do not drink alcohol for 8 weeks.
- Avoid tobacco entirely; nicotine impairs ulcer healing.

DIET
- Resume a regular diet as tolerated.
- Avoid foods that reproducibly cause dyspepsia.

FOLLOW-UP
- Follow up with gastroenterology in 3 weeks.
- Follow up with your primary care physician in 1 week.
- Laboratory visit for complete blood count in 7 days.
- Repeat endoscopy in 8 weeks to confirm healing.

RETURN TO THE EMERGENCY DEPARTMENT IMMEDIATELY FOR:
- Haematemesis, or vomit that looks like coffee grounds
- Melaena, or black tarry stool
- Frank blood per rectum
- Syncope, near-syncope, or palpitations
- Dyspnea on exertion that is new
